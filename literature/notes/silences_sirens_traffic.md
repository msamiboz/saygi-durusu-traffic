# Prior work on mass commemorative silences / remembrance sirens and traffic or observable behaviour

Scope note: about 22 web searches and fetches (English, Turkish, Polish, Korean), done 2026-11-10. Google Scholar, YÖK Ulusal Tez Merkezi and the DergiPark search interface could not be queried directly (they are not reachable through the search tool). DergiPark and Scholar content was found only through general web search. **Main finding: I found no quantitative study, academic or journalistic, that measures traffic speed, flow, delay or driver compliance during any synchronized commemorative silence or siren, in any country.** The closest quantitative "behavioural footprint" evidence is electricity-demand drops that grid operators report during Remembrance Day silences (Ontario, UK). Everything else is descriptive news coverage, official guidance, or ritual and memory scholarship.

## Q1. Prior quantitative work on 10 Kasım 09:05 saygı duruşu and traffic in Turkey

### Takeaway
I found nothing quantitative. Turkish coverage is uniformly descriptive ("hayat durdu", "trafik 1 dakika durdu"). It gives no speeds, counts, navigation-app data, or İBB/UKOME/Emniyet traffic figures. The only scholarly treatment found is a cultural-memory article that mentions the siren and the car horns. As far as these searches show, the paper's measurement would be the first.

### Cited Findings
- Capital (10 Nov 2021, 83rd anniversary): traffic at Ankara's busiest points stopped for about one minute at 09:05. Some drivers got out and some honked along with the sirens. **No figures, no data source, no İBB/police traffic statement.** The headline says "Köprüde" but the body names no bridge. — [Capital](https://www.capital.com.tr/haberler/tum-haberler/ataya-saygi-koprude-trafik-1-dakika-durdu)
- Typical national coverage, all qualitative: D-100 traffic stopped at Şirinevler (Istanbul) and in İzmit, and motorway traffic stopped at many points. — [BirGün 2023](https://www.birgun.net/haber/ataturk-icin-09-05te-hayat-durdu-tum-yurtta-anildi-482573); [T24 İzmit](https://t24.com.tr/haber/10-kasimda-izmitte-hayat-durdu,744513); [T24 Ankara](https://t24.com.tr/haber/ankarada-saat-0905te-hayat-durdu,744733); [DHA](https://www.dha.com.tr/gundem/saat-9u-5-gece-hayat-durdu-2755499); [Habertürk](https://www.haberturk.com/turkiye-saygiyla-ayaga-kalkti-2214859)
- English-language explainers say the stop rests on custom rather than legal obligation. They are descriptive and contain no data. — [Türkiye Today](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/nation/why-turkiye-comes-to-a-standstill-at-905-am-every-november-10-3232088); [Substack explainer (low credibility, blog)](https://learnturkishwithseda.substack.com/p/november-10-in-turkiye-why-do-people)
- A searched combination of "10 Kasım + Yandex Navigasyon / trafik yoğunluğu / 09.05" returned no navigation-app or traffic-index analysis of 10 Kasım, only the ceremonial news above. — searched; no hit (see Gaps)
- Scholarship: an article in *Çağdaş Türkiye Tarihi Araştırmaları Dergisi* (ÇTTAD) XIX/38 (2019), on DergiPark, discusses the 09:05 siren and the car horns that accompany it as a moment of collective memory. It also traces how 10 Kasım changed from a mourning day into a commemoration week. It is historical and qualitative, with no traffic measurement. — [DergiPark ÇTTAD article 657259](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/cttad/article/657259) (*author name and exact title not verified: seen only through search snippets; page not fetched*)
- That article's bibliography reportedly lists two master's theses on 10 Kasım history: B. Şimşek (2007), "Türkiye'de 10 Kasımlar (1938-1950)", and Y. Tatar Hotancı (2017), "Demokrat Parti Döneminde 10 Kasımlar". Both are history of commemoration, not traffic. — via [DergiPark ÇTTAD](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/cttad/article/657259) (*not verified on YÖK Tez Merkezi*)

### Inferences
- The paper can plausibly claim to be the first quantitative estimate of the 10 Kasım traffic stop. Phrase the claim as "to our knowledge", because YÖK Tez and the DergiPark full-text search were not queried directly.
- News accounts support the assumed mechanism (drivers stop in-lane, some exit vehicles and honk, roughly one minute, highways included). They can be cited as qualitative motivation for a full-stop-equivalent framing.

### Gaps
- No İBB, UKOME, Emniyet or KGM statement with traffic figures for 09:05 on 10 Kasım was found.
- No Yandex, Google or TomTom data-journalism on 10 Kasım was found.
- YÖK Ulusal Tez Merkezi and DergiPark could not be searched natively. A manual check with the terms "saygı duruşu", "10 Kasım trafik" and "anma törenleri trafik" is recommended before submission.

## Q2. Israel: Yom HaZikaron and Yom HaShoah sirens

### Takeaway
Drivers stopping on highways (including Ayalon and Highway 1) is well documented in news and photos. The National Road Safety Authority issues official stopping guidance. I found no study measuring compliance, speed, flow or Waze data during the sirens. The ritual is analysed in anthropology (Handelman & Katz), but not quantitatively.

### Cited Findings
- National Road Safety Authority guidance for the 2-minute 10:00 Yom HaShoah siren (Israel National News, 7 Apr 2013): plan to stop about 10 minutes before the siren at a parking area. Otherwise pull onto the right shoulder with hazards on and stand away from the road, preferably behind a barrier. Pedestrians should not assume all drivers will stop. **No accident or compliance statistics.** — [Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/166833)
- Similar authority guidance appears in the Jerusalem Post. — [JPost, "Road safety during Yom HaShoah siren"](https://jpost.com/breaking-news/road-safety-during-yom-hashoah-siren-309002) (*not fetched*)
- Incident reporting: two people were injured on Highway 1 when a driver stopped in a live lane, not on the shoulder, for the Yom HaShoah siren (April 2023). — [Yeshiva World](https://theyeshivaworld.com/?p=2182047); Jewish Press URL from search now returns 404 (*single incident, not systematic data*)
- Descriptive and photo evidence of cars stopping on the Ayalon and on highways. — [TPS](https://tps.co.il/glance-post/cars-stand-still-in-israel-in-memory-of-the-holocaust/); [JTA 2026](https://www.jta.org/2026/04/14/israel/israelis-pause-for-a-different-kind-of-siren-the-one-marking-holocaust-memorial-day); [Al-Monitor 2016](https://al-monitor.com/originals/2016/05/israel-sirens-holocaust-day-memorial-day-fallen-soldiers.html)
- A siren sounded early in Tel Aviv and people stood at attention. This anecdote illustrates that the trigger is the sound itself. — [JPost](https://www.jpost.com/Israel-News/Too-soon-Memorial-Day-siren-sounds-early-Tel-Aviv-stands-at-attention-589042)
- Waze: the only link between Waze and Memorial Day found is a feature showing memorial sites. No Waze analysis of siren stops was found. — [JPost via search](https://www.jpost.com/breaking-news/israel-comes-to-standstill-as-siren-marks-beginning-of-remembrance-day-351308)
- Scholarship: Don Handelman & Elihu Katz, "State ceremonies of Israel: Remembrance Day and Independence Day", in D. Handelman, *Models and Mirrors: Towards an Anthropology of Public Events* (Cambridge UP 1990; Berghahn 1998 reissue). This is the classic ritual analysis of the siren-opened ceremonies. It is qualitative and contains no traffic data. — [Berghahn open-access chapter PDF](https://mail.berghahnbooks.com/downloads/OpenAccess/HandelmanMoebius/HandelmanMoebius_05.pdf) (*fetch returned 403. The chapter itself was not read, and the URL may point to a different Handelman volume, so treat the link as unverified. The bibliographic entry comes from my own knowledge and is not verified this session.*); a [Taylor & Francis chapter page](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/9781351293921/chapters/10.4324/9781351293921-5) describes the same ceremonies, but its authors were not confirmed.
- Not found: any Vinitzky-Seroussi work on siren compliance. Her known work is on Rabin commemoration, which is unrelated to traffic. *Not verified this session.*

### Inferences
- Israel is the closest institutional analogue (a nationwide siren with drivers stopping on highways, and a road-safety authority telling people to stop beforehand). Yet it appears unmeasured, so the paper's design could be pitched as transferable to Israel.
- The Israeli guidance to stop about 10 minutes before the siren implies that anticipatory slowdowns are possible. That supports the paper's use of an hourly window and checks for pre-siren effects.

### Gaps
- No compliance share (fraction of drivers stopping), no measured duration, and no accident statistics from the Road Safety Authority were found.
- Hebrew-language sources were not searched.

## Q3. Poland: 1 August 17:00 "Godzina W" (Warsaw)

### Takeaway
Widely reported: sirens sound for about one minute at 17:00 and cars, buses and trams stop. I found no ZTM report, traffic-data analysis or academic measurement. Polish searches on "ruch samochodowy zatrzymanie" returned only unrelated police material.

### Cited Findings
- Interia: "Warszawa stanęła", with sirens sounding and the city stopping. — [Interia](https://wydarzenia.interia.pl/mazowieckie/news-wybila-godzina-w-warszawa-stanela-w-miescie-zawyly-syreny,nId,23523077)
- Polsat News (80th anniversary, 2024): pedestrians, cars, buses and trams stopped. The sirens use the public-warning system symbolically. — [Polsat News](https://www.polsatnews.pl/wiadomosc/2024-08-01/80-rocznica-wybuchu-powstania-warszawskiego-o-godzinie-w-zawyly-syreny-w-stolicy/)
- RMF24: "silent for 70 seconds". A museum director says the custom had faded in the 1990s and has since revived. — [RMF24](https://www.rmf24.pl/fakty/news-zamilkna-na-70-sekund-dla-powstancow,nId,1478162)
- Other descriptive coverage: [Forsal](https://forsal.pl/artykuly/1061779,w-godzine-w-zawyly-syreny-w-holdzie-dla-powstancow-warszawskich.html); [Bankier](https://www.bankier.pl/wiadomosc/Warszawa-oddala-hold-powstancom-warszawskim-w-godzinie-W-8163128.html); [Gość Warszawski](https://warszawa.gosc.pl/doc/2615385.W-godzine-W-zawyly-syreny)

### Inferences
- The Polish case is a second "city stops for about one minute" event at a fixed annual time. It is a natural external-validity or replication candidate (Warsaw has open traffic and ZTM GTFS-RT data), but no one appears to have done it.
- The reported revival after the 1990s suggests compliance varies over time. For 10 Kasım, year-specific estimates may be worth reporting.

### Gaps
- ZTM and Warsaw traffic-management (ZDM) statements with figures were not found and may exist only in Polish press releases that were not surfaced.

## Q4. Other countries (UK/Commonwealth, China, South Korea, Japan) and outcomes or methods used anywhere

### Takeaway
The only quantitative measurements of a synchronized commemorative silence found anywhere are grid operators' electricity-demand drops: Ontario IESO (about 350 MW in 2013, about 235 MW in 2021, about 290 MW reported in one year) and UK National Grid (1996, "a city the size of Leeds"; 500 MW at the 2022 Queen's funeral silence). These are press figures, not peer-reviewed analyses. China (Nanjing, 13 December) and South Korea (6 June, 10:00) have institutionalised vehicle stops, in Korea at police-designated intersections. Neither has any traffic measurement. Japan 3/11 turned up nothing on traffic.

### Cited Findings
- Ontario IESO, 2021: about 235 MW drop "at the eleventh hour" during the Remembrance Day silence, "a city the size of Waterloo". No duration or method is stated. — [IESO](https://ieso.ca/Sector-Participants/IESO-News/2021/11/Electricity-Demand-Drops-as-Ontario-Observes-Moment-of-Silence)
- Ontario IESO, 2013: about 350 MW drop at 11 a.m., confirmed by an IESO spokesperson, who likened it to demand swings during Olympic hockey. — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/961743/ontario-power-demand-plunges-on-remembrance-day)
- About 290 MW drop reported for another year (year not confirmed). — [APPrO magazine](https://magazine.appro.org/news/ontario-news/6108-1578966619-moment-of-silence-results-in-drop-in-electricity-demand.html) (*not fetched; year unverified*)
- UK 1996: National Grid said demand fell by about a Leeds-sized city's worth as factories stopped machinery. — [BBC Ceefax archive page, 11 Nov 1996](https://al.zerostem.io/~al/teletext/bbc2/1996-11-11-0002.1/111.html) (*archival teletext mirror; not fetched*)
- UK, Queen Elizabeth II's funeral (2022), 2-minute silence: about 500 MW drop. — [inkl](https://inkl.com/news/huge-power-drop-recorded-across-uk-during-the-queen-s-funeral); [StudyFinds](https://studyfinds.com/queen-elizabeth-funeral-power/) (*secondary; National Grid primary not located*)
- China, Nanjing National Memorial Day (13 Dec, since 2014 by law): at about 10:00/10:01 air-raid sirens sound and downtown drivers stop and honk for one minute. Expressways, viaducts and tunnels are reportedly exempt. Roads near the memorial are closed. No traffic data. — [The Nanjinger](https://thenanjinger.com/news/nanjing-news/stand-by-for-1-minute-of-honking-horns-on-13-december/); [The Nanjinger 2021](https://www.thenanjinger.com/?p=19285); [China Daily 2020](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202012/13/WS5fd59d7ba31024ad0ba9b7c4.html); [Shine](https://www.shine.cn/news/nation/2012141568)
- China's 4 April 2020 COVID national mourning (10:00, 3 minutes, cars and trains stopped and horns sounded) is known from my own background knowledge only. **No source was retrieved this session, so it is unverified.**
- South Korea, Memorial Day (6 June, 10:00, 1-minute siren): pre-event reports say vehicles pause at 225 major road points nationwide, 18 of them in Seoul (e.g. Sejongno intersection, Gwanghwamun, Samseong Station). This implies a police-managed stop at designated intersections rather than a universal one. The siren is not an air-raid alert. No measurement was found. — [Kukinews 2023](https://www.kukinews.com/article/view/kuk202306050119) (*fetch returned 403; content from search snippet*); [Wikitree](https://www.wikitree.co.kr/articles/1055626); [Seoul Shinmun 2016](https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/2016/06/06/20160606800031)
- Japan 3/11 moment of silence (14:46): **no traffic-related source found** (searched only indirectly; no hit).
- Outcomes and methods used anywhere: grid-demand time series, which are press-reported operator observations with no counterfactual. No speeds, counts, phone mobility data, surveys or systematic observation of driver compliance were found for any commemorative siren. — synthesis of the above

### Inferences
- The electricity-demand evidence is the best methodological precedent. It shows that a synchronized silence leaves a measurable aggregate footprint, but those reports use simple before/after eyeballing. The paper's within-day difference-in-differences against same-weekday controls, with randomization inference, would be a methodological step up from anything in this literature.
- The Korean "designated intersections" model and the Chinese expressway exemption differ from the Turkish practice, where drivers stop anywhere, including highways. That institutional contrast is worth one sentence in the paper.
- No source gives a compliance share or a stop duration beyond the nominal 1 to 2 minutes. The paper's full-stop-equivalent minutes would be the first quantitative proxy for compliance-weighted stopping.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper on electricity demand during commemorative silences was found. There is a broader literature on "TV pickup" and event-driven demand that was not searched here.
- Japan, Australia/NZ (ANZAC), the Netherlands (4 May Dodenherdenking, 20:00) and US "National Moment of Remembrance" were not searched in depth. The Dutch 4 May two-minute silence, where traffic and public transport stop, is a plausible further analogue and was not checked.
- Google Scholar, YÖK and Hebrew sources were not searched natively. Absence of results is strong for news and data-journalism, and weaker for theses and grey literature.
