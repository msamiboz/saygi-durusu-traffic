# Measuring Istanbul's 09:05 stop fills an empty niche

Six research notes covered news, grey literature, transport economics, econometrics, İBB documentation and Turkish memory studies. None of them turned up a quantitative estimate of traffic delay during any synchronized commemorative silence or siren, in Turkey or anywhere else. **The paper's 0.73 full-stop-equivalent minutes (50–70 km/h cells, p = 0.008) appears to be the first behavioural measurement of compliance with the 10 Kasım ritual, and possibly the first traffic-based measurement of any national moment of silence** ([silences note sources: Capital](https://www.capital.com.tr/haberler/tum-haberler/ataya-saygi-koprude-trafik-1-dakika-durdu); [IESO](https://ieso.ca/Sector-Participants/IESO-News/2021/11/Electricity-Demand-Drops-as-Ontario-Observes-Moment-of-Silence)). The closest precedents are grid operators' press reports of electricity dips during the UK, Ontario and royal-funeral silences. Those reports have no counterfactual and no uncertainty, so the design adds formal counterfactual inference to a literature that has none. On method, the design sits in the "event day vs same weekday in adjacent weeks" tradition (Redelmeier and coauthors). Published reanalyses show that this tradition's weak point is the small control pool: [Zhang & Aronow (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-016-2148-6) found that an election-day crash effect vanished against 200 placebo Tuesdays. Widening the placebo pool, using controls that are robust to a linear trend (Nov 10 is always the second of four dates), and adjusting for multiplicity across speed classes are therefore the top priorities. Under Bonferroni or Holm across five classes, the 0.008 result survives and the 0.034 result does not. The measurement side backs the lower-bound reading: if stationary pings are filtered, a full stop barely registers in speed averages, and the 0.73-minute estimate is smaller than the 1-minute full-stop benchmark under correct measurement. **TomTom is not available, so the feasible sub-hour validation is İBB's own minute-level sensor speeds, which researchers have obtained on request** ([Takak et al. 2021](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/1590162)).

## Synchronized silences leave footprints that no one has counterfactually measured

The pattern holds across all six notes. Turkish coverage of 10 Kasım is uniformly qualitative ("hayat durdu", "trafik 1 dakika durdu"). It reports D-100 traffic stopping at Şirinevler and motorway traffic halting, but gives no speeds, counts or İBB/Emniyet figures ([BirGün 2023](https://www.birgun.net/haber/ataturk-icin-09-05te-hayat-durdu-tum-yurtta-anildi-482573)). Israel, Warsaw's Godzina W, Nanjing's 13 December and Korea's 6 June all have institutionalized vehicle stops, and none has a traffic measurement. The only quantitative footprints anywhere are grid-demand drops: **about 620 MW** in the UK on Armistice Day 2010, **about 500 MW** beyond the broadcast-driven fall during Queen Elizabeth II's funeral silence, and **about 235 MW** in Ontario in 2021 ([carbonfeednews copy of BBC report](https://carbonfeednews.blogspot.com/2010/11/bn-uks-two-minute-silence-cuts-power.html); [inkl](https://inkl.com/news/huge-power-drop-recorded-across-uk-during-the-queen-s-funeral); [IESO](https://ieso.ca/Sector-Participants/IESO-News/2021/11/Electricity-Demand-Drops-as-Ontario-Observes-Moment-of-Silence)). Each is converted into a "city the size of X" metaphor without stating a baseline. The paper's conversion, minutes = 60·(1−e^D), belongs to this same tradition of turning an aggregate into a participation equivalent. The difference is that its assumptions can be written down and its inference is formal.

The historical record shows that the stop is old, voluntary and norm-driven, which justifies reading it as revealed participation. In Istanbul on 10 November 1939, "tramvaylar ve araçlar saat 09.05'te oldukları yerde beş dakika durmuş". Official mourning ended in 1988, but the silence, horns and sirens continued "herhangi bir zorunluluk olmadığı halde" ([Uzun 2019](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)). No statute obliging drivers to stop was found. Survey evidence sits at a ceiling: **89.7%** supported the 09:05 silence in a 1988 PİAR poll, and 86–92% report gratitude to Atatürk in recent MetroPOLL surveys ([Uzun 2019, fn. 103](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084); [Diken](https://www.diken.com.tr/metropollden-ataturk-anketi/)). A behavioural measure avoids both the ceiling and social-desirability bias.

The data set itself has a thin record. The two peer-reviewed uses of the İBB hourly geohash data are single-cell forecasting exercises. Both treat NUMBER_OF_VEHICLES as a flow count, which the official dictionary does not support: it defines the column as "the number of different vehicles in the relevant geohash area in the given hour" ([Utku 2023](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/2521442); [Özkurt et al. 2025](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4793568); [İBB data dictionary](https://data.ibb.gov.tr/en/dataset/hourly-traffic-density-data-set/resource/00d72836-d035-462d-a66e-408883216195)). No causal or event-study use of the data set was found.

## Five gaps the paper fills, and how to say so

The notes support five distinct gaps. **First, no quantitative measurement of any synchronized commemorative stop exists in traffic data.** That holds for Turkey, Israel, Poland, China, Korea and the UK. The only numbers anywhere are grid-operator press figures, with no stated baseline or uncertainty ([IESO](https://ieso.ca/Sector-Participants/IESO-News/2021/11/Electricity-Demand-Drops-as-Ontario-Observes-Moment-of-Silence)). **Second, there is no behavioural measure of 10 Kasım compliance.** Surveys measure attitudes at a ceiling, and the only item on the 09:05 silence dates from 1988 ([Uzun 2019](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)). **Third, the İBB hourly geohash data have never been used for causal or event-study work.** The two peer-reviewed uses forecast a single cell and misread the vehicle count ([Utku 2023](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/2521442)). **Fourth, no paper formalizes how a k-minute coordinated stop maps into hourly probe aggregates.** The probe note derives the mapping. Under space-mean speed with vehicles present all hour and no stop filtering, 60·(1−e^D) returns exactly k. Each departure (filtering, time-mean averaging, partial presence, composition change) biases the estimate toward zero, while queue discharge pushes it the other way ([Knoop et al. 2009](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:4b4e33a5-06b4-4bad-90f6-79f7c9fdee01)). **Fifth, the one-day-event literature lacks formal inference for minutes-long shocks.** Electricity, water and CDR studies use same-weekday baselines without tests. The Redelmeier crash studies use two control days and have been overturned when the pool was widened ([Zhang & Aronow 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-016-2148-6)).

The headline numbers support the lower-bound framing. A 1-minute full stop measured correctly lowers hourly log speed by 0.0168, and a 2-minute stop by 0.0339. The 0.73-minute estimate corresponds to a log-speed effect of about −0.0122, and the 0.45-minute estimate to about −0.0075. Both are below even the 1-minute benchmark, although current practice is a two-minute silence from 09:05 to 09:07 ([Güneydoğu Ekspres](https://www.guneydoguekspres.com/10-kasim-sirenleri-neden-caliyor-ataturk-icin-neden-0905te-saygi-durusu-yapiliyor-siren-sesi-tehlike-uyarisi-mi)). This is what the probe literature predicts if stationary pings are dropped: a stop then registers only through braking, creep and discharge. The null in congested cells fits the same story. Time-mean bias is largest at low speeds ([Knoop et al. 2009](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:4b4e33a5-06b4-4bad-90f6-79f7c9fdee01)), and a ≥ 1 km/h filter removes most of the extra stopped time where traffic already crawls. Queue theory predicts the opposite for genuine delay: near capacity, discharge from a 2-minute stop at q/C = 0.8 lasts about 8 minutes. So the congested-cell null is better read as a measurement limit than as non-participation. That reading is an inference and has not been tested.

### Draft positioning paragraph for the introduction

> Every year at 09:05 on 10 November, Turkey observes a moment of silence for Atatürk. Sirens sound, and drivers across the country stop their vehicles, a practice documented in Istanbul since the first anniversary in 1939 and sustained without legal obligation since official mourning ended in 1988 (Uzun 2019). Synchronized national silences of this kind exist in Israel, Poland, China, South Korea and the Commonwealth, yet to our knowledge their behavioural footprint has never been measured with a counterfactual. The only quantitative evidence consists of grid operators' press reports of electricity dips during the British and Canadian Remembrance silences, which state no baseline and no uncertainty. Survey evidence on the Atatürk commemoration measures attitudes that sit near a ceiling, not behaviour. We provide what we believe is the first causal estimate of the traffic delay caused by a commemorative siren. We use İBB's hourly geohash-level probe speeds for Istanbul (2020–2024), a within-day difference-in-differences that compares the 09:00 hour with adjacent hours on 10 November against the same contrast on same-weekday control dates, and stratified randomization inference over all 1,024 within-year assignments of the treated date (Fisher 1935; Young 2019; Hagemann 2019). We convert log-speed effects into full-stop-equivalent minutes, a transparent analogue of the participation equivalents grid operators report, and show why hourly probe aggregates that exclude near-stationary observations yield a lower bound. In free-flowing 50–70 km/h cells the siren costs at least 0.73 full-stop-equivalent minutes per vehicle-hour (p = 0.008). The effect is smaller in 30–50 km/h cells and undetectable in congested cells, where the measurement is least able to register a stop. Beyond the commemoration itself, the paper offers a template for measuring civil rituals as revealed behaviour, alongside flag-display counts (Dinas, Martínez & Valentim 2024) and the effects of shared national experiences (Depetris-Chauvin, Durante & Campante 2020). It also offers a template for inference on minutes-long, once-a-year shocks, where the small control pools of earlier one-day-event studies have proved fragile (Zhang & Aronow 2016).

Two phrasings in this draft are deliberate hedges that the notes require. "To our knowledge" and "we believe" are there because YÖK Tez, DergiPark full text and Google Scholar could not be searched natively. Do the manual YÖK and DergiPark search for "saygı duruşu", "10 Kasım trafik" and "İBB trafik yoğunluk" before submission. Before the multiplicity adjustment is run, the 30–50 km/h result should be described as suggestive, not significant.

Several lower-priority checks follow from specific sources. The first is to report the raw 09h DiD separately from the within-day contrast and to test the 10h hour for post-stop rebound. Earth Hour smart-meter work found rebound ([APPAM](https://appam.confex.com/appam/sc20/webprogram/Paper34260.html)), and catch-up congestion in the 10h control hour would attenuate the contrast. The second is covariate-adjusted RI that permutes residuals after a weather regression ([Rosenbaum 2002](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1042727942)). The third is a placebo-law simulation that plants fake "treated" dates in October to estimate the pipeline's false-rejection rate ([Bertrand et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355304772839588)). The fourth is a heuristic correction from time-mean to space-mean speed using (MAX−MIN)/4 as a dispersion proxy. It follows [Rakha & Zhang 2005](https://trid.trb.org/view/775960), but the range proxy is the notes' own construction and should be labelled as such. A naive cell-clustered t-test belongs in an appendix only, to show how far cell-level SEs overstate precision ([Young 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjy029)). Two extensions would turn the paper from a measurement exercise into a social-science contribution. One is heterogeneity by district CHP/AKP vote share, which [Goldman 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/21520844.2018.1485347) predicts. The other is running the same pipeline on iftar, Istanbul's other synchronized city-wide shock, as an external validation ([Türkiye Today](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/turkiye/istanbuls-traffic-grinds-to-halt-as-ramadan-begins-in-one-of-worlds-most-congested-cities-126496/)). On interpretation, the flag-count literature warns that public acts mix preference with norm-following and peer effects ([Dinas et al. 2024](https://cadmus.eui.eu/entities/publication/7610a146-9008-55f4-a290-148e7d877267)). In dense traffic, one stopped car stops the cars behind it, so the estimate should be framed as aggregate, collective participation and not as a share of drivers who chose to stop.

TomTom MOVE Traffic Stats appeared in the probe note as a planned 15-minute validation source. That plan is now infeasible because the account was blocked, so TomTom enters the paper only as literature: its Traffic Index as motivation, and its documentation as evidence that commercial providers do not disclose how they handle stationary probes. The probe note expected traversal-based commercial travel times to give a larger estimate than ping-based speeds. That expectation can now be tested only with İBB's own minute-level RTMS or segment data, so the data request in check 7 does double duty.

## Conclusion

The literature changes how the paper's main weakness should be read. The small effect sizes and the congested-cell null are not mainly a power problem. They are what a measurement instrument that drops near-zero speeds would produce for a two-minute synchronized stop, so the most valuable single action is an answer from İBB about the 1 km/h floor and the averaging rule. A confirmed filter would turn "lower bound" from a caveat into a quantified gap. If İBB also supplies minute-level speeds, the paper could report a bracket with the hourly probe estimate as the floor.

On inference, the design is stronger than anything in its own lineage, but its exposure is specific and known: a fixed second-of-four position and a three-day control pool, the exact features that sank earlier one-day-event findings. Running checks 1 to 3 before circulating the draft costs little. If the 50–70 km/h result survives adjacent-date controls, a ±10-week placebo pool and Holm adjustment, the paper can make an unusually clean claim: the first counterfactual measurement of a national moment of silence, with inference that addresses the published critiques of the method it builds on.
