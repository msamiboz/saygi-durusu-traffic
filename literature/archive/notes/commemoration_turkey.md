## Q1. History of the 10 Kasım 09:05 commemoration, siren practice, legal basis, and stopping vehicles

### Takeaway
The 09:05 standing silence has run every year since the first anniversary, 10 November 1939. It began as a CHP/Halkevi and school programme, and the first Istanbul ceremony already had trams and vehicles stopping for five minutes. Car horns plus ship and factory sirens were a regular feature by the 1940s–50s. Official mourning ended in 1988, but the silence, the horns and the sirens carried on. The best historical source (Uzun 2019) describes them as continued "without any obligation" (herhangi bir zorunluluk olmadığı halde) by a large part of society. I found no statute or circular that obliges drivers to stop. That makes the traffic stop a largely voluntary, norm-driven act, which supports the "behavioural participation" reading.

### Cited Findings
- **Key source [V]:** Hakan Uzun, "Türkiye'de 10 Kasım Törenleri: 'İlk Matem' 10 Kasım 1939'dan, 'Atatürk Haftası' ve 'Matemsiz Atatürk'ü Anma' Günlerine," *Çağdaş Türkiye Tarihi Araştırmaları Dergisi* XIX/38 (2019, Bahar), pp. 135–170. Archival and press history covering 1939–1988. — [DergiPark PDF](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)
- The first annual programme, for 10 November 1939, was published in *Cumhuriyet* on 5 November 1939 and is reproduced in Uzun's Ek-1. It called for a meeting at 09:05 in all Halkevleri (or Party centres where there was no Halkevi) with "ayakta beş dakika saygı susması", the same five-minute standing silence at 09:05 in all schools, black-framed front pages, and the radio scheduled so the commemoration fell at 9:05. **Note:** the programme as reproduced does not mention sirens or traffic. It was a party, school, press and radio programme. — [Uzun 2019, pp. 168–169](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)
- **Earliest documented vehicle stop:** in Istanbul on 10 November 1939, "tramvaylar ve araçlar saat 09.05'te oldukları yerde beş dakika durmuş", shops stayed closed for hours, and boats in the harbour lowered their flags to half-mast (Uzun citing *Cumhuriyet*, 11 İkinciteşrin 1939). — [Uzun 2019, p. ~140](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)
- In the 1940s, mourning practice meant "arabalar kornalarını, fabrikalar ve vapurlar da sirenlerini çalmışlardır", entertainment venues closed, and flags flew at half-mast. — [Uzun 2019, p. 149](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)
- Norm enforcement in the 1950s: in 1951 the Egyptian consulate was accused in the press of insensitivity for not lowering its flag. In 1959 a Greek freighter that "did not lower its flag or join the sirens" was confronted by a crowd on the quay until it lowered the flag. — [Uzun 2019, p. ~153](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)
- Atatürk Haftası (10–16 November) was introduced after the 27 May 1960 coup on the initiative of MBK members. For schools it was formalised by the Talim ve Terbiye decision of 7/9/1963, no. 2016-10. From 1960 to 1988: "saat 09.05'te tüm yurtta saygı duruşunda bulunulmuş, arabaların kornaları ile birlikte vapurların, gemilerin, fabrikaların sirenleri çalınmış". — [Uzun 2019, pp. ~155–156](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)
- In 1988, under Turgut Özal, the 50-year official mourning (resmi yas) was abolished. On 10 November 1988 the 09:05 silence, car horns and ship and factory sirens still happened, while entertainment venues stayed open. Uzun argues that these behaviours persist "as the result of the public's wish to keep a tradition", not as mourning. The abstract says some behaviours were "herhangi bir zorunluluk olmadığı halde toplumun geniş bir kesimi tarafından benimsenerek sürdürülmüş". — [Uzun 2019, pp. 135, 160–161](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)
- Uzun's conclusion lists, among the unchanged practices since 1939, the 09:05 silence, horns plus ship and factory sirens, and "tam o anda neredeyse ülkede hayatın tamamen durması". — [Uzun 2019, p. 161](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)
- A local-press study of Burdur, 1938–1955, reports a first ceremony on 21 November 1938 with a five-minute silence. — [Academia.edu, "Burdur Yerel Gazete ve Dergilerinde 10 Kasım Atatürk'ü Anma Günü"](https://www.academia.edu/31633180/Burdur_Yerel_Gazete_ve_Dergilerinde_10_Kas%C4%B1m_Atat%C3%BCrk%C3%BC_Anma_G%C3%BCn%C3%BC) [P: author and venue not confirmed]
- A popular news piece says the 1939 Interior Ministry programme decided on nationwide sirens at 09:05, citing Uzun. — [Mepanews](https://www.mepanews.com/10-kasim-torenleri-ne-zaman-basladi-dunyada-benzer-uygulamalar-var-mi-80620h.htm). **Contradicted** by the programme text reproduced in Uzun (Ek-1), which has no sirens and was a CHP/Halkevi programme. Treat the news claim as unreliable.
- Current practice: sirens sound 09:05–09:07 with a two-minute silence (one minute for other occasions). Drivers stop, get out of their cars, or sound their horns. These are not disaster sirens. — [Güneydoğu Ekspres](https://www.guneydoguekspres.com/10-kasim-sirenleri-neden-caliyor-ataturk-icin-neden-0905te-saygi-durusu-yapiliyor-siren-sesi-tehlike-uyarisi-mi); [Milli Gazete](https://www.milligazete.com.tr/10-kasimda-sirenler-neden-calar-saat-kacta-calacak-10-kasim-sirenleri-kac-dakika-surer-neden-saygi-durusu-yapilir); [tr.wikipedia, 10 Kasım Atatürk'ü Anma Günü ve Atatürk Haftası](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_Kas%C4%B1m_Atat%C3%BCrk'%C3%BC_anma_g%C3%BCn%C3%BC_ve_Atat%C3%BCrk_haftas%C4%B1) (news and encyclopaedia, not scholarly)

### Inferences
- Stopping vehicles at 09:05 is at least as old as the ritual itself (Istanbul 1939). It is a long-standing, collectively sustained norm, not something introduced recently.
- Since 1988 there has been no mourning obligation, and I found no traffic-law requirement to stop. The slowdown therefore measures voluntary (norm-driven) compliance more than legal compliance. This is the core justification for the "revealed participation" reading.
- The siren is now mainly a coordination signal (civil-defence or municipal sirens plus horns). The traffic stop may partly reflect "can't move because the car ahead stopped" rather than individual intent. That suggests framing the estimate as aggregate or collective participation, not as a share of individuals.

### Gaps
- I found no primary document (law, regulation, Başbakanlık or İçişleri genelgesi) that sets out (a) when the municipal or civil-defence siren at 09:05 became standard, or (b) any instruction to drivers. Annual valilik or Kaymakamlık "10 Kasım anma programı" circulars likely exist. Check Resmî Gazete, AFAD (siren system), and İstanbul Valiliği announcements. Not found in this session.
- Uzun's footnote 6 and the 1940s sections may name the first year sirens were systematically used. I did not read the whole article. ## Q2. Key scholarly works: Atatürk cult, Kemalism as civil or political religion, commemoration politics, AKP-era polarization

### Takeaway
Anthropology of 1990s–2000s Turkey (Navaro-Yashin, Özyürek, Çınar, Ökten) shows Atatürk's image and 10 Kasım shifting from top-down state ritual to a voluntary, privatised, often oppositional performance of secularist identity. Goldman (2018) carries this into polarization under the AKP. A Turkish-language literature debates whether Kemalism is a "civil religion" (Bellah/Rousseau) or a "political religion". Together these support reading 10 Kasım participation as a partisan or identity-laden act, and they predict heterogeneity by district secular/CHP vs. AKP composition.

Taking part in the 09:05 stop is near-universal, consensual behaviour in public, so heterogeneity may be modest. That makes it a conservative test.
- Civil-religion framing (Bellah) suits the paper: the 09:05 stop is a synchronised, liturgy-like act of the national "civil religion". "Political religion" framing emphasises the state-imposed origins of 1939 (party/Halkevi programme).

- I did not read the Ökten chapter or the relevant Özyürek and Navaro-Yashin chapters, so I cannot quote what they say about traffic stops or sirens specifically.

## Q3. Comparative anthropology and sociology of moments of silence and sirens

### Takeaway
The closest comparators are Israel's Yom HaZikaron and Holocaust Day sirens, where traffic visibly stops, and the British two-minute Armistice silence. Classic works are Handelman (with Katz) on Israeli state ceremonies, Gregory on the interwar Armistice silence, and Vinitzky-Seroussi on fragmented commemoration and on silence. None of them, as far as I found, *quantifies* how many people stop.

### Cited Findings
- **[V] Don Handelman, *Models and Mirrors: Towards an Anthropology of Public Events*, Berghahn Books, 1998 edition.** Chapter 9 (with Elihu Katz) covers Israeli state ceremonies of Remembrance Day and Independence Day. — [Berghahn](https://www.berghahnbooks.com/title/HandelmanModels). [U] The first edition is Cambridge University Press, 1990 (not verified this session).
- **[V] Adrian Gregory (1994), *The Silence of Memory: Armistice Day, 1919–1946*, Berg** (Legacy of the Great War series; reissued 2014). A secondary summary says it traces the two-minute silence back to the "three minutes' pause" practised in South Africa in 1918. — [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Silence_of_Memory.html?id=vPTCAgAAQBAJ); [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Silence-Memory-Armistice-1919-1946-Legacy/dp/1859730019). The South Africa origin claim is from a secondary summary and should be checked in the book.
- **[V] Vered Vinitzky-Seroussi (2002), "Commemorating a Difficult Past: Yitzhak Rabin's Memorials," *American Sociological Review* 67(1): 30–51.** DOI 10.1177/000312240206700102. Introduces "fragmented" commemoration: different times and spaces for different audiences. — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/commemorating-a-difficult-past-yitzhak-rabins-memorials-3/); [CiteDrive](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/commemorating-a-difficult-past-yitzhak-rabins-memorials)
- **[V] Vered Vinitzky-Seroussi & Chana Teeger (2010), "Unpacking the Unspoken: Silence in Collective Memory and Forgetting," *Social Forces* 88(3): 1103–1122.** Distinguishes overt from covert silence. Silence can serve memory, not only forgetting. — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/unpacking-the-unspoken-silence-in-collective-memory-and-forgettin-3/)
- **[V] Vinitzky-Seroussi (2009), *Yitzhak Rabin's Assassination and the Dilemmas of Commemoration*, SUNY Press.** — [SUNY Press](https://www.sunypress.edu/isbn/9781438428314)
- Israel: Yom HaZikaron opens with a nationwide siren during which people "stop what they are doing and stand in silence for two minutes". A second siren sounds at 11:00. The day was enacted in law in 1962. — [Israeli Embassy, Washington](https://embassies.gov.il/washington/NewsAndEvents/Pages/Israel-Memorial-Day.aspx). Press reports describe traffic in Tel Aviv slowing to a standstill. — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/Israel-News/Too-soon-Memorial-Day-siren-sounds-early-Tel-Aviv-stands-at-attention-589042)

### Inferences
- Like Turkey, Israel has a siren-triggered, synchronised stop in which drivers leave their cars. Israel's traffic data could in principle support a replication or comparison. The Israeli rituals are tied to war dead and are less partisan-coded, though Haredi non-participation is a well-known (unquantified here) axis of heterogeneity.
- Vinitzky-Seroussi's "fragmented commemoration" fits Turkey: official Anıtkabir ceremonies coexist with privatised and oppositional participation.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published study that counts or estimates the share of people or vehicles stopping during Yom HaZikaron sirens or the UK two-minute silence. [U] Handelman and Katz may describe the Israeli siren qualitatively; this was not checked.
- [U] Possible further comparators not verified: the Dutch 4 May two-minute silence (Dodenherdenking); Poland's 1 August Warsaw Uprising "W-hour" sirens (Godzina W), where traffic also stops.

## Q4. Survey and observational measures of participation in Atatürk and 10 Kasım rituals

### Takeaway
There is very little direct measurement. The only survey I found that asks specifically about the 09:05 silence is a 1988 PİAR poll for *Sabah*: 89.7% said a silence should be held at 09:05. Recent MetroPOLL polls measure attitudes ("gratitude to Atatürk" ~86–92%), not behaviour. I found no observational count of how many people or vehicles stop. This is a genuine gap that the paper's traffic-based measure fills.

### Cited Findings
- **PİAR poll for *Sabah*, 10 November 1988** (reported in Uzun 2019, fn. 103):
  - 49.1% had heard of the decision to commemorate without mourning.
  - 74.5% approved ending mourning; 25.4% disapproved.
  - 80% wanted festivals and sports events held in Atatürk's memory.
  - **89.7% believed a saygı duruşu should be held at 09:05, 7.8% did not, 2.5% had no opinion.**
  - [Uzun 2019, p. 159](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084). Sample size and design are not reported.
- **MetroPOLL, November 2021:** 92% said "yes, I feel gratitude (şükran) to Atatürk"; 73% said Atatürk's value has recently come to be better appreciated. — [Onedio](https://onedio.com/haber/metropoll-anketi-halkin-yuzde-92-si-ataturk-e-sukran-duyuyor-1020709); [Diken](https://www.diken.com.tr/metropollden-ataturk-anketi/). A different MetroPOLL report gives 86.4% for the same question in an undated November survey. — [Cumhuriyet](https://www.cumhuriyet.com.tr/turkiye/metropoll-anketi-halkin-yuzde-86si-ataturke-sukran-duyuyor-2139348). The year of the 86.4% figure is unverified, and the two figures may come from different years. MetroPOLL reports are typically broken down by party vote. Check the original reports for partisan splits.
- **KONDA:** asked what comes to mind with "Cumhuriyet". 37% answered Atatürk (40% among youth). This is an attitude and salience measure, not participation. — [Cumhuriyet on KONDA](https://www.cumhuriyet.com.tr/turkiye/konda-arastirma-nin-anketi-ortaya-koydu-cumhuriyet-denince-halkin-aklina-ne-geliyor-2551652)
- Historical observational evidence is purely qualitative: press descriptions of trams stopping in 1939, and of "life nearly stopping" at 09:05. — [Uzun 2019](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/879084)

### Inferences
- A 1988 benchmark of about 90% normative support, together with 86–92% "gratitude" in the 2020s, implies the norm is near-consensual at the attitude level. Variation in *behaviour* (the traffic stop) may therefore show differences in intensity and salience that surveys cannot pick up because of ceiling effects and social desirability. This is a strong argument for a revealed-behaviour measure.
- Survey social desirability around Atatürk is likely high: law 5816 criminalises insulting Atatürk [U: law not verified this session]. Behavioural measures avoid this problem.

### Gaps
- I found no survey item asking "did you stop or stand at 09:05" and no observational count (CCTV, traffic, mobile data) of compliance in Turkey or elsewhere.
- I did not check Ipsos, Optimar, ORC or Gezici polls on Atatürk. Party breakdowns of MetroPOLL items were not retrieved.

## Q5. Behavioural or "revealed" measures of nationalism, and links to local political composition

### Takeaway
Economics and political science increasingly use field behaviour to measure nationalism and the effects of national rituals: Fourth of July exposure (Madestam and Yanagizawa-Drott), football victories (Depetris-Chauvin, Durante and Campante), and counts of flags on balconies (Dinas, Martínez and Valentim; Parravano et al.). Flag counts are the closest methodological analogue. They are a visible, public, costless-but-normed act aggregated by street, and they have been linked to local politics and norm shifts. I found no study using traffic or mobility data to measure ritual compliance. The paper's design appears novel.

### Cited Findings
- **[P] Andreas Madestam & David Yanagizawa-Drott, "Shaping the Nation: The Effect of Fourth of July on Political Preferences and Behavior in the United States."** Working paper: IGIER WP 399 (2011); HKS Faculty Research WP RWP12-034 (2012). Uses rain on 4 July as exogenous variation in childhood participation. One rain-free Fourth before age 18 raises adult Republican identification by about 2% and Republican vote by about 4%, and turnout by 0.9%. **I found no journal publication. It is not in QJE as the brief suggested. Cite it as a working paper.** — [IGIER WP 399](https://ideas.repec.org/p/igi/igierp/399.html); [HKS RWP12-034](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ecl/harjfk/rwp12-034.html); [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-a438-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b)
- **[V] Emilio Depetris-Chauvin, Ruben Durante & Filipe Campante (2020), "Building Nations through Shared Experiences: Evidence from African Football," *American Economic Review* 110(5): 1572–1602.** DOI [10.1257/aer.20180805](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20180805). After national-team victories, respondents are 37% less likely to identify primarily with their ethnic group and 30% more likely to trust other ethnicities. Countries that barely qualified for the Africa Cup of Nations had about 9% fewer conflict episodes.

Displays rose from negligible to more than three per street after the October 2017 Catalan referendum. ### Inferences
- Flag-count studies (Dinas et al.; Parravano et al.) are the best template. Like the 09:05 stop, they treat a public, socially visible, weakly enforced act as revealed attachment. They also stress that such acts mix preferences with *norms and peer effects*. This caveat applies directly: a driver may stop because others stop, and in dense Istanbul traffic one vehicle stopping slows everyone. Interpret district-level heterogeneity as variation in local *norm strength*, not individual preference.
- Dinas et al.'s finding that displays respond to political shocks suggests a trend analysis over 2020–2024. Possible interpretations include COVID-era 2020–21 traffic, the centenary of the Republic in 2023, and the 2023 elections. These are hypotheses, not findings.
- For district heterogeneity, use YSK district-level vote shares: CHP vs. AKP and MHP in the 2019 local and 2023 general elections. Prior literature (Özyürek, Goldman) predicts stronger participation in secular and CHP districts. Nationalist (MHP/İYİ) voters may also take part strongly, so CHP share alone may be the wrong proxy. Consider "AKP share" or an index.

### Gaps
- I found no study linking Turkish district or neighbourhood vote shares to any Atatürk-ritual behaviour (attendance at ceremonies, flag or Atatürk-poster display). The heterogeneity analysis would be a first.
- I found no prior use of traffic, mobility or electricity data to measure compliance with a moment of silence anywhere. [U] There may be related work using mobility or electricity data around televised events or religious rituals (e.g. Ramadan iftar electricity load, or Super Bowl water use, the "flush" effect). That could be cited for the "high-frequency utility data reveals synchronised collective behaviour" idea, but none of it was verified here.

### Short synthesis for the writer
1. **History:** The 09:05 silence dates to the first anniversary, 10 November 1939, a CHP/Halkevi and school programme. Istanbul trams and vehicles already stopped for five minutes that day. Horns and ship and factory sirens were standard from the 1940s. Official mourning ended in 1988, but the silence, horns, sirens and stopping continued as a non-obligatory popular tradition (Uzun 2019). No legal duty for drivers to stop was found.
2. **Meaning:** Since the 1990s the Atatürk cult has been privatised and has become a marker of secularist identity in a polarized polity (Navaro-Yashin 2002; Özyürek 2004, 2006; Ökten 2007; Goldman 2018). Scholars debate whether it is civil religion or political religion (Karakuş 2023; Atalay).
3. **Measurement:** Survey data show near-consensual attitudes: 89.7% supported the 09:05 silence in 1988 and 86–92% express gratitude toward Atatürk in the 2020s. Data on behaviour is missing. The traffic-slowdown estimate would be, as far as this search found, the first quantitative behavioural measure of compliance with the 10 Kasım ritual, and possibly with any siren-triggered moment of silence.
4. **Framing:** Place it alongside revealed-nationalism work (flag counts: Dinas et al. 2024, Parravano et al. 2015; ritual exposure: Madestam and Yanagizawa-Drott WP; shared experiences: Depetris-Chauvin et al. 2020). Heed their caution that public acts mix preferences and social norms.
